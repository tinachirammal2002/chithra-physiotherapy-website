ARTHRITIC PHYSIOTHERAPY

Understanding Your Arthritis

Our physiotherapists can help you to understand what happens to your joints and muscles when you have arthritis. Understanding your arthritis will help you to manage its effects.

Arthritis can cause pain in one particular part of the body or more widespread joint and muscle pain. Medications will help, but a physiotherapist can tell you about other methods of pain relief that work alongside your medications.

STAY ACTIVE

Keeping Active Is Important

Keeping active is very important when you have arthritis. Many people are afraid that exercise will increase their pain or cause further damage to their joints.

However, your joints are designed to move and the muscles and tissues around them can become weaker if they are not used. This can cause your joint to become unstable and may reduce your mobility and independence.

Exercise can increase your general fitness, help you to lose weight or maintain a healthy weight, improve your general mobility and make you feel more self-confident.

PHYSIOTHERAPY TECHNIQUES

Some of the Techniques We Apply

Our physiotherapists use appropriate techniques based on the individual's condition, symptoms and rehabilitation needs.

Ice Packs

Ice packs can be used to soothe hot and swollen joints.

Heat Packs

Heat packs help to relax tense and tired muscles.

Splinting

Splinting swollen or painful joints may be helpful, for example during a flare-up of rheumatoid arthritis.

TENS

Transcutaneous Electrical Nerve Stimulation works by blocking pain messages to the brain and altering the perception of pain.

Graded Exercise

Exercise starts slowly and increases in small steps to strengthen muscles and joints and improve fitness.

Massage

Massage can help muscles relax and make joint movement more comfortable.

Electrotherapy

Techniques such as ultrasound and low-level laser therapy can help stimulate the healing process and therefore reduce pain.

Manipulation

Manipulation can help to improve the range of movement in your joint.
