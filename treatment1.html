NECK & BACK CARE

Back and Neck Pain

The human back is composed of a complex structure of muscles, ligaments, tendons, disks and bones. The segments of our spine are cushioned with cartilage-like pads called disks. Problems with any of these components can lead to back pain. In some cases of back pain, its cause is never found.

Our clinic conducts a comprehensive examination to establish the underlying cause of your discomfort. In our experience, pain can often be referred from weakness or immobility of a distant muscle group or joint. We aim for effective treatment in addition to long-term control for your particular complaint.

Most people have heard of the deep core trunk muscles, often the target of Pilates programs, and work hard to strengthen them. However, sometimes it is the timing that is out, not the strength.

Latest research has shown that the anticipatory timing and co-activation of the deep core muscles such as transversus abdominis and multifidus, together with superficial muscles such as the obliques and rectus abdominis, can become disrupted with back pain.

Similarly, headaches and chronic neck pain can also respond to strengthening of the deep muscles in the neck.

Chronic pain states are sometimes actually driven by the brain in an attempt to protect you from further harm, resulting in what is called Central Sensitisation. Our clinic incorporates the latest science of retraining your brain into your home exercise routine, which can assist many sufferers of chronic back and neck pain, sciatica or various nerve injuries.

COMMON CAUSES

Back & Neck Pain Can Be Caused By

We design the most appropriate sessions according to the underlying cause and individual needs of each patient.

Strain

Some of the most common causes of back pain include strained muscles, strained ligaments and muscle spasms.

  • Strained muscles
  • Strained ligaments
  • Muscle spasm

Causes of Strains & Spasms

Everyday movements and incorrect lifting techniques can place excessive stress on the back.

  • Lifting something improperly
  • Lifting something too heavy
  • Abrupt and awkward movements

Ruptured Disks

Each vertebra in the spine is cushioned by disks. If a disk ruptures, increased pressure on a nerve can result in back pain.

Bulging Disks

A bulging disk may place pressure on nearby nerves and can contribute to back pain and other symptoms.

Sciatica

Sciatica may cause sharp or shooting pain travelling through the buttock and down the back of the leg.

Arthritis

Osteoarthritis can affect joints including the hips, lower back, knees and hands. In some cases, spinal stenosis may develop.

Abnormal Curvature

An unusual curvature of the spine may increase the likelihood of experiencing back pain. Scoliosis is one example.

Osteoporosis

Bones including the vertebrae can become brittle and porous, making compression fractures more likely.

Poor Mattress Support

A mattress that does not adequately support the body and maintain spinal alignment may contribute to back discomfort.

Poor Posture

Everyday activities, prolonged positions and poor posture can contribute to neck and back problems.
